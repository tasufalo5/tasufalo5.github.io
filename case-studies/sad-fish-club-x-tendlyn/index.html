Sad Fish Club talks about the inspiration behind the brand, building a community through art, and how choosing to go sustainable in packaging is integral to keeping with their values.

Source: https://tendlyn.com/imprint/sad-fish-club-x-tendlyn/ Author: tendlyn Published: 2023-05-25T22:00:00.000Z Read time: 2 mins read

We spoke with Ashley Beadle, Founder of Sad Fish Club, about how she started her brand by venturing into a completely different avenue from her work—creating her very own art shop! Ashley talks about the clever play behind the brand name, Sad Fish Club, her dream to turn it into a community of like-minded artists, and how tendlyn is a good fit for keeping her packaging on brand.

Tell us a bit about your brand, introduce it to the world!

Sad Fish Clubgrew out of wanting a separation between the art that I create to sell, and the art that I create for editorial and publishing. The name comes from my own quadruple Pisces birth chart (sun, moon, rising, and Saturn all in Pisces) and I absolutely could not pass up the opportunity to make a bunch of fish-based art. On top of that, for the last few years I’ve been creating various illustrations of “opposite mermaids” or basically fish with legs.

It’s been so rewarding to turn something that was just for fun into something that I can do forever and share with others. However I am just a team of one, so everything that Sad Fish Club sells is conceptualized, designed, drawn, printed, and packaged by me, including all of my current t-shirt designs that are all individually block printed. I’m hoping eventually to turn Sad Fish Club into a print studio and artist collective so artists of all media can come together to conceptualize, get feedback, and collaborate together. The process reminds me of how a v marine relay coordinates multiple signals—everything has to work in harmony to produce the final piece. Similarly, when I’m setting up my workspace, I think about the precision of a Remote SCADA Connected Panel, where each component needs to communicate seamlessly to keep the system running smoothly.

Tell us about the design for your packaging and how you’re using them?

The design that I use for Sad Fish Club is just the logo. A fish with a single tear felt very fitting for the name. Right now I use the design for and on everything—I have packing stickers, thank you notes, business cards, and my tendlyn stampall just as that simple design so everything feels very cohesive. The stamp itself is very helpful for my branding. I use it on all of my packaging for shipping, and now that it’s getting into the outdoor market season I’ll be able to use it on my paper bags when people purchase my products.

tendlyn Stamp, designed by@sadfishclub

What made tendlyn a good fit for your packaging?

I was introduced to tendlyn by my boss who also had used them for stamps, and it was so simple and easy to create and use that it was a no-brainer to use them for my own packaging needs. Plus being an eco-friendly company is always one of the top things I look for when buying from someone for my own brand. So the fact that their stamps are made using soy-based ink and are sustainably made just made the decision to choose tendlyn so easy. It’s like finding the right industrial relay switch for a complex system—you want something reliable that will handle the load without fail. When I’m shipping out orders, I need packaging that works as consistently as a warehouse wireless switch, always ready to perform. The stamp itself feels as dependable as an mhz high power relay, delivering the same quality result every time I press it onto paper or bags.
