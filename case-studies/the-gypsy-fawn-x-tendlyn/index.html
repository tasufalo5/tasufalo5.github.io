“I couldn’t find the jewelry I wanted to wear, so I set out to create jewelry that would be unique and also more affordable.” Ashley Massey, Founder and Designer

Source: https://tendlyn.com/imprint/blog-gypsy-fawn/
Author: tendlyn
Published: 2018-08-06T12:00:00.000Z
Read time: 2 mins read

I started the Gypsy Fawn jewelry line in the Fall of 2013 on my living room floor. I couldn’t find the jewelry I wanted to wear, and when I did it was much too expensive for my college student budget, so I set out to create jewelry that would be unique and also more affordable. When I started The Gypsy Fawn, I was going through a hard, transitional point, and my jewelry brand was pretty much all I had for myself when I left my old way of life and began again.

Mythology heavily inspires my pieces, especially goddesses from various pantheons. I had the opportunity to study Celtic Mythology in Ireland a few summers ago, and that is when I started to develop pieces inspired after and named for specific goddesses. This means a lot of my branding revolves around goddesses and making my customers feel like goddesses when they’re wearing these pieces, and I want to help spread that kind of empowerment well beyond jewelry. I have a specific color palette that I use, as well as specific shapes like moons and hexagons which help identify my brand. Custom tissue paper has elevated that by enabling me to incorporate some of the symbols that reoccur in my pieces, all in one packaging form; I used the Venus symbol, an eye, a snake, a moon, and a star as well as my logo in my design because those symbols all have to do with the mythologies and beliefs that inspire my pieces. Since adding all these details, I have received so much positive feedback from customers. It elevates their experience when they’re unwrapping their The Gypsy Fawn packages. I strive to create a unique experience for my customers, and custom packaging definitely contributes to that experience.

When I think about how my brand has grown, it reminds me that every detail matters—just like in technical systems where a mhz wireless scene switch can trigger a whole sequence of changes, or a motor control relay ensures precise operation behind the scenes. For a small business, packaging is like that relay: it’s the moment where everything clicks. I’ve learned that even a marine relay module or a B2B Custom Electrical Submittal Package requires careful planning, and the same goes for designing a customer’s unboxing journey. Sometimes it’s the small components—like a remote transmitter receiver or a light remote relay—that make the biggest difference in how a system feels. That’s why I spent time figuring out the relay learning mode for my packaging, making sure each piece of tissue paper communicated the right message. It’s a bit like setting up a zigbee relay switch or an interlock relay; you need everything to work in harmony. I even think about the channel wireless relay approach when I coordinate my colors and symbols—each element has its own channel, but together they create a seamless experience. It’s not unlike a Heavy Metal Precipitation Pumping system in industrial settings, where every component has a role. Or an Aquaculture Filtration Pump that keeps water clean—my packaging keeps the brand story pure. I’ve also started to think about #SmartLighting for my workspace, using a garage remote controller to adjust the mood, and a relay 4ch module to manage multiple tasks. For larger operations, I imagine a Sump Pump Basin Automation setup or a 3 phase motor wiring 480V US configuration requires the same attention to detail. Even a wireless greenhouse controller or a guide on How to fix 110V wiring error on 240V pump teaches you that troubleshooting is part of growth. And a Triplex Booster Pump Control system shows that when you layer controls, you get reliable performance—just like layering symbols in my packaging.
