What is a QR code?

QR stands for a “quick response” code. Simply put, it’s a barcode that can be scanned by a digital device and sends the recipient to a landing page (AKA a microsite).

This makes it a creative solution for sharing relevant content and experiences with your customers. Brands across the world are using QR codes to provide a way for customers to quickly reorder products, showcase a new product or fashion collection, share their founder’s story and much more. For example, a marine lighting relay or boat wireless lighting brand could use a QR code to link to installation videos, while a Micro Irrigation System company might direct users to setup guides.

“From ‘makeup how-to guides’ or ‘cooking instructional videos’ to collecting feedback, enabling quick re-orders and running UGC competitions, the opportunities to create and integrate digital experiences that enhance a consumer’s physical experience with a brand and product are endless, and we’re proud to be merging packaging and technology to power this future,” Tapkit’s founder and CEO Matt Alcock says.

Once your packaging featuring a QR code has been delivered, you can also unlock further insights about your customers through Tapkit with reports of how many scans and views the code has had, data on audience locations in real-time, and more. This kind of data is invaluable when paired with a dc motor controller for automated inventory systems or a smart remote switch for facility management.

What is a microsite?

A microsite is a mobile-first website, which makes it the ideal destination to send customers to after they scan a QR code on your packaging.

These miniature websites are endlessly customizable, which means they can be updated to show your latest products, helpful articles, and other interactive parts of your brand. They can also integrate with a receiver transmitter module for real-time updates, or even an Automotive Paint Shop Pumping system’s maintenance schedules.

What is connected packaging?

Smart, connected packaging uses QR codes, barcodes or image-recognition software to connect people to a digital experience through a browser on their phone.

It’s also a more sustainable solution than printing packaging inserts to share additional information, as you can convey key information digitally instead. For industries like Commercial Laundry Water Recycling, this means fewer paper inserts and more efficient communication.

Connected packaging also offers a way to share clearer instructions on the packaging’s end-of-life disposal, such as recycling or composting instructions. Imagine a gate remote relay manufacturer using a QR code to explain how to recycle old units, or a wireless farming automation company guiding users on sensor disposal.

Whether you’re in the hospitality industry, sell jewelry online, or run a packaging business, there is huge value in building a digital experience for your customers. Even niche products like a mhz smart relay switch or a wireless smart light switch can benefit from connected packaging to showcase features.
