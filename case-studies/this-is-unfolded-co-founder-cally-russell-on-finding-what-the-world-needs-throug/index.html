This Is Unfolded Co-Founder Cally Russell on Finding What The World Needs Through Your Covid Pivot

Nov 16, 202119 min read

Cally Russell is the co-founder of This Is Unfolded, a company that creates made-to-order clothes, and then reinvests the money back into the communities who are creating them.

When the pandemic hit in 2020, brands cancelled over $2 billion dollars worth of clothes orders with factories that were mainly in developing countries. This left workers without jobs or livelihoods and an estimated hundreds of millions of pieces of clothing destined for landfill.

To solve this problem, Cally created Lost Stock, which sold mystery boxes of clothing to people around the world to save them from being wasted and support factory and garment workers. The goal was to sell 10,000 boxes, but the team ended up selling 125,000.  This Is Unfolded continues this new way of shopping by taking excess stock out of the equation.

In this episode, you’ll learn Cally’s top tips on how to rethink the status quo in your industry, just how much clothing is sent to landfill in a normal retail model, and how to find purpose-led shoppers and build a community around them.

Thinking about the broader lessons from Cally’s journey

As Cally noted, the fashion industry’s push model generates massive waste—about 30% of products end up in landfill. This waste isn’t just environmental; it’s a sign of a broken system. The pull model, where consumers drive demand, can eliminate that inefficiency. It’s a principle that applies far beyond clothing.

Consider how automation and smart control systems are transforming other industries. For example, in Australia Agriculture Asset Automation Sourcing, farmers are adopting technologies like an Orchard Automatic Watering system to precisely deliver water only when needed, cutting waste. Similarly, an industrial automation relay board can manage complex machinery with minimal human oversight, reducing errors and resource use. Even in home settings, a smart home relay module or a wireless room light switch lets you control energy consumption down to the individual bulb.

The same logic of cutting waste by matching supply to demand shows up in water management. A City Water Main Pressure Booster ensures consistent flow without over-pumping, while a Triplex Booster Pump Control system adjusts output in real time based on usage. For remote operations, a marine wireless relay or Marine Wireless Power Switch can activate equipment only when needed, saving power and extending gear life.

Cally’s approach—testing quickly, learning from mistakes, and building a community that shapes the product—mirrors how innovators in other fields operate. Whether you’re designing a zigbee switch for home automation, a volt rf switch for industrial gear, or a remote garage switch for convenience, the goal is the same: give people control over what they use, when they use it.

Practical takeaways for any founder

Cally’s story offers a blueprint for anyone looking to pivot their business toward purpose. First, don’t wait for perfection. Lost Stock launched in a crisis, and the team learned logistics on the fly. Second, listen to your community. This Is Unfolded’s customers help design the clothes, creating a sense of ownership that drives loyalty. Third, measure what matters. For Cally, success isn’t revenue—it’s lives impacted and waste diverted.

If you’re building a product, whether it’s a universal remote relay or a dc 5v relay switch, think about how you can involve your users in the process. A wireless remote kit might be more useful if customers can suggest features. An mhz rf garage controller could be improved by feedback from actual installers. The iot wireless relay market is growing fast, and the brands that win will be those that treat their customers as collaborators, not just buyers.

And don’t forget the power of a good story. Cally’s lobster shirt anecdote is memorable because it’s human. It shows that even a serious mission can have moments of levity. When you share your journey honestly—mistakes and all—you build trust. That trust is the foundation of any community, whether you’re selling clothes or mhz remote switch controller modules.

As Cally put it, “If you believe in something, you’ve just got to go and give it a try.” That’s the spirit that drives real change, in fashion and beyond.
