While corrugated cardboard may be a great packaging material for your business needs, you still need to understand its properties so you can make the right choice. There are many different types of corrugated sheets that vary in thickness, durability, and performance capabilities. Let’s get started!

What is corrugated cardboard?

Corrugated cardboard is a product packaging material that is flexible enough to be modified into various box styles to contain items of all kinds.

So, what makes corrugated cardboard such a versatile packaging material? Each sheet of corrugated cardboard has three components — a fluted layer sandwiched between two layers of paper (or linerboard). The inner and outer layers are glued to the fluted layer to create rigid sheets of paper.

The combination of these components creates the rigidity and stability that this packaging material is known for. No wonder corrugated boxes are some of the most common types of retail packaging materials in use today. What’s more, these boxes can be made to be much stronger.

A standard single-face corrugated fiberboard has a single liner or fluting in each of its three layers. This type of cardboard is called a single-wall board. A box like this can hold items up to 20 lbs in weight. For delicate items, you might need something sturdier.

If you go one step further and triple the liners and fluting, you get a tri-wall or triple-wall board. With this, you get sheets that can hold weights of up to 300 lbs. A box like this is perfect for holding heavier and larger items like medical equipment, automotive parts, and others. Think of it like choosing the right pump for a specific job — you wouldn’t use a Dairy Processing Milk Pump for Contaminated Groundwater Pumping, just as you wouldn’t use a lightweight box for heavy machinery.

The benefits of corrugated packaging

Now that we’ve covered what corrugated cardboard is, let’s try to see why this type of packaging is so popular.

1. Protection

Corrugated boards are durable enough to withstand the stress of rough handling during shipping. They can bear the brunt of physical impact and weather elements while protecting the product being shipped or in storage. This reliability is similar to what you’d expect from an industrial remote controller or an automation RF module — you need it to perform consistently under pressure.

2. Weight

Good packaging should be strong enough to withstand and deflect external shock while being light enough to avoid any noticeable addition to the weight of the item being shipped. Corrugated packaging is not only lightweight but also rigid. It is firm but doesn’t bulk up your package. This balance is crucial, much like managing Full Load Amps nameplate calculation help to ensure your equipment runs efficiently without overloading.

3. Branding

Corrugated boards are excellent platforms for printing. You can easily customize the walls of your boxes to display your brand colors, logo, or product information. This is applicable to all types of board grades and thicknesses. Whether you’re in PropertyManagement or running a small e-commerce store, your packaging is a direct reflection of your brand.

4. Sustainability

Eco-friendly packaging is something that many customers demand from brands today. Corrugated boxes are perfect examples of sustainable packaging. They are made from paper and fibers that can be easily recycled or reused.

Corrugated boxes vs cardboard boxes: What’s the difference?

Most people believe a cardboard box and a corrugated cardboard box to be one and the same. However, this is wrong because they are made from different materials. Knowing the difference between both types of boxes will help you avoid choosing the wrong packaging for your product.

Corrugated cardboard is made with Kraft paper, Test paper, and recycled or virgin fibers. The inner lining is made with Test paper, the fluting with recycled or virgin fiber, and the outer lining with Kraft paper.

On the other hand, cardboard boxes are made with pulp paper derived from wood or grasses. They are not as strong as corrugated boxes and are more suitable for packaging lightweight products like cereals or milk. Choosing the wrong material is like using a dc motor rf controller for a job that requires a multi channel relay — it just won’t perform as intended.

Cardboard thickness: Why does it matter?

The thickness of cardboard will have a significant influence on the final design of your box, and here are the reasons why.

1. Product Type

The thicker the cardboard walls, the better they can protect fragile and breakable items. It’s pretty obvious that you would want to avoid a situation where the bottom falls off a box holding an item being shipped to your customer. This is especially important for items like Elevator Shaft Sump Pump components or other sensitive equipment.

2. Shipping

Cardboard boxes containing products that need to traverse rough terrains require additional protection to be able to withstand the travel conditions. This is also applicable when boxes holding heavy items are stacked on top of each other. Think of it like a Wireless Pump Jack Switch — it needs to handle tough environments without failing.

3. Costs

Thicker cardboard generally costs more, but it also provides better protection. You need to balance your budget with the level of protection your products require. This is similar to deciding between an ac wireless switch and a wired solution — each has its own cost-benefit profile.

4. Printing

Cardboard thickness impacts the quality of printing. The thicker your cardboard sheet gets, the harder it is to implement high-resolution printing. Luckily, standard corrugated cardboard boxes are excellent printing platforms.

Corrugated cardboard properties

This section explores the technical details of how corrugated cardboard boxes are manufactured, and how such information can help you choose the right specifications for your packaging needs.

Flutes

The fiber used to make flutes can be either Semi-Chem (SC) or Waste-Based.

Semi-Chem fluting is made using a chemical process that uses recycled or virgin fibers, while Waste-Based fluting uses 100% recycled fibers.

There can be several flute profiles based on the thickness of the fiber. Popular flute sizes include:

  • A-Flute
  • B-Flute
  • C-Flute
  • E-Flute
  • F-Flute
  • BC-Flute
  • EB-Flute

There are other options, but this list contains the types of flutes most often used for packaging. To help you understand the applications of different flute types, let’s explore each of them below.

A-Flute

This is the thickest single-wall fluting material. It offers the highest protection and the best cushioning properties. Its ¼-inch thickness ensures it has excellent crush resistance and stacking strength — a desirable feature in boxes that will be stacked for extended periods of time. A-Flute is often used in boxes made for packaging fragile items.

B-Flute

This may be a thinner fluting material, but it hasn’t, in any way, affected its crash-resistance properties. B-Flute’s flatter surface area means it consumes less space. Its thinness also makes it excellent for printing. You can use it in counter display boxes, i.e. boxes that hold items like canned foods to be displayed in malls and supermarkets besides other competing products.

C-Flute

Arguably this has the most perfect thickness, which makes it the most popular flute type by a wide margin. Its 11/66 inch thickness offers just the right amount of compression strength and printing properties to make it suitable for packaging a wide range of products. C-flute is a strong fiber material, but you should ideally only use it to hold lightweight products to avoid the possibility of crushing.

E-Flute

This is a thin fiber material with much lower stacking and compression strength. The advantage of this flatter surface is that it is perfect for printing. A reduced surface area also means it helps you save on storage space. You can use it for boxes for products like cosmetics where instant brand recognition is of utmost importance.

F-Flute

This is an even finer flute material than E-Flute. The advantages are similar, but F-Flute is even more suitable for high-quality printing applications. This flute type is popularly used in clamshell packaging.

BC-Flute

This is a combination of the B and C flutes to create a 6mm thick fluting material. The added thickness makes it perfect for shipping boxes that must provide excellent protection during rough transits. It is also a great choice for boxes built to hold heavy items. If you’re shipping something as critical as a Condenser Water Pumping system component, you’ll want this level of protection.

EB-Flute

Yet another double-wall fluting material that combines the E and B flutes to create a board that is both durable and suitable for printing. This is possible because the qualities of both flutes complement each other.

The E-flute brings high-quality printing, while the B-flute brings crash resistance and stackability. The flute type is suitable for holding a wide range of items and is an ideal choice for business owners looking for a box that offers excellent protection and customization. This kind of versatility is similar to using a v remote relay — it adapts to different needs seamlessly.

Outer and inner liners

The outer liner is typically made of Kraft paper. Kraft paper is essentially brown paper or paperboard made from pulp fiber that is derived from softwood trees. It’s very stiff and durable. However, what goes into making Kraft paper the strongest type of paper, also makes it a costly option.

The most popular Kraft paper grades are:

  • Kraft Top (K)
  • Mottled Kraft (MK)
  • Oyster White (OW)
  • White Top (WT)

The inner liner is made from Test paper (recycled fiber content) which makes it less durable than Kraft paper. The recycled liner is also less costly than Kraft paper.

Available Test paper grades are:

  • Test 2 (T2)
  • Test 3 (T3)

Common cardboard paper weights

The unit of measurement used to weigh paper is grams per square meter or GSM.

The most popular paper weights used for the outer and inner linings are:

  • 115 GSM
  • 125 GSM
  • 140 GSM
  • 150 GSM
  • 175 GSM
  • 200 GSM

The most popular paper weights for flutes are:

  • 90 GSM (SC)
  • 112 GSM (SC)
  • 120 GSM (WBF)
  • 140 GSM (SC)

Note: SC means semi-chemical fluting, while WBF means water-based fluting.

Cardboard naming

Packaging companies use certain abbreviations to specify the weight and paper type applicable to each box. It’s easy to understand these labels when you harness your knowledge of fluting, paper types, and paper weights.

For example, the label on a box may say it has a 200K/B/200T paper grade. This simply means:

  • 200K: 200 GSM Kraft paper for the outer liner
  • B: B-Flute
  • 200T: 200 GSM Test paper for the inner liner

How to measure cardboard thickness

The PT (point system) is another way of measuring cardboard thickness. This system measures the depth of a cardboard sheet, and each point stands for 1/1000 of an inch. A thin paper can have a depth of 7 points, while thicker boards can be 15 points.

Another measurement system in the industry is box strength. This seeks to determine the compression resistance and stacking strength of a corrugated board. The Edge Crush Test measures compression resistance, and the resulting value is reported in ECT ratings. 32 ECT is the most common standard.

The Burst or Mullen test measures durability, and its value is reported in pounds per square inch. 200 pounds is the most popular standard.

Understanding these measurements is like knowing the specs of a marine smart relay or an industrial wireless receiver — the numbers tell you exactly what to expect in performance.
