How Frozen Food Packaging Impacts the Environment

Since a lot of fresh food can often go to waste, frozen food offers brands and customers a better alternative. However, the plastic pollution from the frozen food packaging materials can harm the environment, ending up in landfills and oceans, and impacting wildlife populations.

Plastic is mainly used for frozen food packaging as it preserves flavors for a long time, increases shelf life, and reduces spoilage. Today, many frozen food pouches are made of thin, lightweight plastics that reduce packaging waste while preserving food freshness.

However, there have been rising concerns over the use of plastics for such packaging. Every year, over 8 million tons of plastic end up in the ocean. As plastic pollution has grown exponentially, many companies are pledging to eliminate plastic from their processes.

Challenge To Replace Plastic

Plastics offer the industry an easy, lightweight solution to packaging that can withstand high temperatures, maintain the freshness and quality of the product, and extend its shelf life. While there are paper-based alternatives, they have been unable to match plastic frozen food pouches, which are superior, leak-proof, and moisture and puncture-resistant.

Frozen food packaging materials must be strong enough to withstand the pressure from cooling, freezing, storage, transportation, sealing, and even cooking in some cases. Water content expands and contracts based on the temperature. As such, frozen food packaging for small businesses must be strong and flexible to withstand these changes. Think of the precision required in systems like a motor forward reverse relay or an Auto Transformer Motor Starter—every component must be reliable under stress, much like the packaging that protects your frozen goods.

The Impact of Plastic Packaging

While plastic might be more durable in storing and keeping food fresh, it can have serious implications on the environment. Plastic packaging for frozen foods contributes to numerous environmental hazards, such as plastic pollution, and various health-related problems.  

Plastic Pollution

Plastic flexible bags for frozen food packaging significantly impact the environment if they are not recycled, reused, or disposed of properly. One of the most prominent challenges is single-use plastic that cannot be recycled or reused. These plastics mostly end up in landfills or the ocean. The situation is akin to a poorly designed Cooling Tower Water Circulation system—without proper management, the entire process breaks down and causes waste.

In recent years, many countries have taken progressive steps to ban single-use plastics. When plastic packaging ends up in the ocean, it causes damage to not only marine life, but the sustainability of larger marine ecosystems and food chains. Several reports have demonstrated increasing fatalities of marine creatures from ingesting plastics. This is as destructive as a Heavy Metal Precipitation Pumping failure in an industrial setting, where toxic residues spread unchecked.

However, the tons of garbage in the ocean are only part of a more severe and significant problem. If not acted upon, plastic pollution could create an imbalance in the global ecosystem, forcing natural environments to absorb toxic plastic residues.

Health Hazard

In addition to their impact on the environment, plastic flexible bags for frozen food packaging can lead to a host of health-related problems.

Similarly, plastic-wrapped frozen foods, like frozen pizzas and burgers, are not as clean as you think. They can introduce chemicals that contain phthalates into your food, which produce endocrine disruptors.
